# Dream design notes

The dream uses research as artistic inspiration, rather than claiming that all dreams work the same way.

Picard-Deland and colleagues collected dreams across sleep stages from 20 participants. They found that multiple memory sources can coexist in a dream. Their paper discusses familiar events appearing as fragments combined into new scenarios rather than complete replays. [The memory sources of dreams: serial awakenings across sleep stages and time of night](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091095/).

The design translates that into recognizable domestic objects in mismatched settings, changing connections between home and work, and actions that feel understandable despite impossible results. Floating pages change between familiar sentences; a faucet pours upward and releases paper boats; a photograph alternates between a bedroom window and the restaurant; a hanging coat releases leaves. These particular interactions are creative inventions, not scientific claims about what everyone dreams.

All ten interactions are optional. There are no collectible requirements, mandatory jumps or exit-door objectives. The dream stays calm for 45 seconds before its separate corruption timer begins. Interaction effects during that quiet period remain gentle and do not accelerate waking. The dream wakes the player at 90 seconds.

The native and browser versions share the same geometry and numerical cloth/dream-motion equations. Browser tests raycast both red eyes from the bed, frame the entire TV, check hand-to-blanket contact and walk the complete day. Actual rendering and audio still require device playtesting.
